## Scope

This checkpoint continues the GF(2) winning-line incidence investigation from primitives `2, K=4, H=6, W=7`. It does not use solved W/D/L labels and does not insert `28` or `69` as theorem premises.

## Projection falsifiers and corrected cores

The two originally proposed one-axis projections fail exactly:

- column parity restricted to `im(B)` has rank **4**, not 7;
- pure-horizontal-line parity by row restricted to `ker(B)` has rank **5**, not 6.

Natural corrected quotients are:

- cell side: row-plus-column parity restricted to `im(B)`, rank **7**;
- line side: pure-vertical-line parity by column restricted to `ker(B)`, rank **6**, landing in the even-weight column subspace.

Their kernels are both explicitly constructed and both have derived dimension **28**.

## Important symmetry correction

The bare geometric incidence operator `B` has top-bottom reflection symmetry, but Connect4 does not: gravity/support order distinguishes early low cells from later high cells, and the strategic redundancy of later threats is not vertically symmetric.

The corrected cell and line cores have equal fixed dimension under left-right reflection (14 each) but unequal fixed dimension under top-bottom reflection (16 versus 14). Therefore:

- this mismatch falsifies a **B-only** reflection-equivariant identification;
- it does **not** falsify a Connect4-natural identification that is allowed to use gravity/support order.

This distinction is now explicit in the executable control.

## Residual cofactor is a genuine graded complex

Let `C_d` be the GF(2) space generated by every unique `d`-cell residual subset of an original Connect-4 line, and let `partial_d` delete one cell from each `d`-fragment.

For standard 7x6 the unique residual-level counts are:

- `C1`: 42
- `C2`: 282
- `C3`: 232
- `C4`: 69

The finite control proves:

- `partial_4` has rank 69 and is injective;
- `partial_3 partial_4 = 0`;
- if `J3` is ordinary cell incidence of degree-3 fragments, then `J3 partial_4 = B`.

Thus the native residual cofactor really is derivative/boundary-like. However the naive `7 * 3 = 21` contraction is **not** the degree-3 incidence rank: `rank(J3)=42`. Therefore `28 -> 21` is not a theorem of residual arity alone.

## New result: natural perfect dual pairing

For a cofactor fragment `S = line \\ {cell}`, define the gravity/residual bit

```text
g(S) = supportClosureSize(S)
     + componentCountAlongOriginalLine(S)
     + maxRow(S)
     mod 2.
```

This uses only Connect4-native residual geometry and support order. It is invariant under left-right reflection and intentionally not invariant under top-bottom reflection.

Use `g` as a weighted line-to-cell incidence operator and restrict the induced bilinear form to the corrected 28-dimensional cores. The resulting 28x28 pairing matrix has rank **28**.

Therefore the current strongest justified structural statement is

```text
Y_line ~= Y_cell^*
```

through an explicit, nondegenerate, gravity-aware residual pairing.

This is substantially stronger than same dimension. It is also stricter than arbitrary Gaussian basis matching: the pairing is defined before any basis is chosen and survives left-right board symmetry.

## Direct-isomorphism boundary remains open

A direct canonical map `T : Y_line -> Y_cell` is not yet proved.

The obvious ambient dot products do not provide a free self-duality:

- dot product restricted to `Y_cell` has rank 24;
- dot product restricted to `Y_line` has rank 24.

So the perfect pairing canonically identifies `Y_line` with the **dual** of `Y_cell`, but another Connect4-native nondegenerate form or explicit intertwiner is still required to identify it with `Y_cell` itself.

## P / CPC result

Same-column two-ply stutter remains exact zero displacement over GF(2).

For distinct columns, the phase delta `e_a + e_b` lies in the corrected cell quotient's length-4 column-interval sector only for column pairs

```text
(1,5), (2,6), (3,7)
```

using one-based columns. Therefore generic CPC phase transport does not act solely on that quotient sector and cannot yet be described as fixing the 28-core by assumption.

## Nearby-family falsification

For the family `W=2K-1, H=2K-2`, finite controls for `K=2..8` derive `L`, `rank(B)`, and `nullity(B)`. Among those tested members, only **K=4** satisfies

```text
rank(B) - W = nullity(B) - H.
```

The smallest counterexample is `K=2`. This strongly suggests the equal middle dimension is special to standard Connect4 rather than a generic member of the whole parameter family. No all-K formula is claimed from this finite neighborhood.
