Revision 0.8 integrates the broader Connect4/IsoMax corpus after auditing the current 93-claim authority inventory. In addition to the recent control-parity work, it now includes exact q semantics, finite-difference and CPC-potential algebra, the rule-derived non-center opening theorem, perfect-play output algebra and the 61-vs-28 tie-convention distinction, recursive proof antichains, realizability/strategy complexity boundaries, RBA partial-value algebra, support-local exact action-value frontiers, and scoped IsoMax execution consequences.

The paper explicitly discloses AI-agent assistance and states that the AI agent was **not responsible for the core findings**. Joshua Oshiro is credited as author and as the originator of the core conceptual findings and research direction. The IsoGraph system is credited as designed by Joshua Oshiro.

The theorem-contamination firewall remains in force: solved-game knowledge may guide discovery or post-hoc validation, but no production search-elimination theorem may derive authority from solved outcomes, including indirectly through fitted targets, definitions, thresholds, case splits, or upstream lemmas.

The title is deliberately playful. "C = NC?" uses C for Connect Four and is a reference to the style of P-versus-NP notation; it is not a formal claim about the complexity class NC. The paper does not claim that a polynomial generalized Connect Four solution has been established.
