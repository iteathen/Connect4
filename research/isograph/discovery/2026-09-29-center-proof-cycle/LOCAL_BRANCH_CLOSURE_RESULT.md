## Question

The direct residual-orbit graph is smaller than the physical game graph, but
its static structural states are still finer than the recursive action-
unlabelled quotient.

This experiment measures the recursive depth of that remaining collapse.

Round 0 treats every nonterminal residual-orbit state as distinct. Each next
round replaces a nonterminal state by:

~~~text
(rank, mover, SET of previous-round child classes)
~~~

Literal action labels and duplicate equivalent choices are erased. Terminal
types remain fixed.

The process uses no minimax values. It is a pure structural partition closure.

## Interpretation

The recursive quotient is not a W/D/L calculation. It is the least finite
action-unlabelled future-behavior partition generated by repeated child-class
set equivalence on the acyclic residual transition graph.

Once the direct structural graph exists, the exact quotient can be computed
in one reverse topological pass by hash-consing child-class sets. The iterative
rounds above are a discovery instrument that exposes how far branch-local
equivalence must propagate.

Therefore two complexity burdens must remain separate:

~~~text
quotient minimization over an existing finite structural graph
!=
construction size of the structural graph itself
~~~

The former is straightforwardly polynomial in the graph representation.
The latter remains unresolved and may still grow exponentially with board
dimensions.

## Effect of rule-derived implicit assertions

The three local rewrites reduce the 4x4 C4 direct graph from 10,507 to 9,441
states and reduce closure depth from 8 rounds to 7 while preserving the exact
8,242-class partition.

This is evidence that recursive implicit assertions can be compiled into
pre-branch normalization rules rather than left entirely to later fixed-point
