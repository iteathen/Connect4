## 3. What this does and does not factor out

This is an **ownership-preserving local interchange of two complete alternating rounds**. Unlike opposite-color adjacent swaps, it factors out genuine chronology of two owner-labelled rounds while holding original event/owner consequences fixed. It can be chained to identify a trace equivalence of particular guarded complete event words without valuing two future games.

**Not yet a strategic equivalence:** In real adversarial play, p's second chosen move c may depend on observing opponent b. Swapping whole rounds changes what the opponent has observed before choosing d and what p can choose at the root. Equality of endpoint q after TWO FIXED full response pairs does NOT prove the corresponding first moves a,c are both optimal, nor commute the intervening ∀ opponent/∃ mover quantifiers. To eliminate *strategy* branching one needs **interactive confluence**: a source theorem ensuring the opponent's arbitrary responses, p's conditional choices, and first-win termination transport across the square. Positional q_o future congruence AFTER a whole block is weaker than that universal interactive diamond.

An important related research tradition is Paul-André Melliès's work on **asynchronous games and interactive forward/backward confluence**, which studies when interleaving strategies can be represented causally. This literature is a methodological analogy, not a theorem authorizing a Connect4 strategic quotient (Melliès, *Asynchronous Games 2*, Theoretical Computer Science 358 (2006), 200–228; Castellan et al., *Games and Strategies as Event Structures*, LMCS 13(3), 2017).
